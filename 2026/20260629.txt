[Gregory Sadler](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/10-famous-lines-by-philosophers-should), a philosopher who teaches the classics online, goes through ten famous lines by philosophers and finds most of them traveling without their books, often under the wrong name. "Well begun is half done" is a proverb Aristotle quotes in the *Politics*. "Power tends to corrupt" is Lord Acton, in a letter, not Burke. "Hell is other people" is said by Garcin, a character in Sartre's *No Exit*, not by Sartre speaking for himself. A line cut loose from its text usually ends up saying something its author didn't.

That's true, and no book suffers from it more than the Bible. One of the most quoted verses on gym walls is [I can do everything through Christ, who gives me strength](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/philippians_4-11-13.txt#v13). Paul isn't talking about winning. He's writing from prison, and the sentence before it says he has learned to be content with plenty or with little. The strength he means is the strength to have enough.

The remedy is the one Sadler asks for: read the paragraph around the line. A verse, like an aphorism, was written to be read next to its neighbors.

!!Read the paragraph before you quote the line.!!
