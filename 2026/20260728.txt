Angus Fletcher, who studies narrative cognition, argues that the most common mistake about intelligence is assuming the brain works like a computer. Computers process forward: input, operation, output. Humans think in stories, oriented around agents moving through time. The architecture is different in a way that matters practically.

One consequence: optimism isn't a forward projection. You don't sustain yourself through difficulty by imagining a positive outcome. You get there by [what experience has already deposited](post.html?p=2026/20260707.txt) — past moments of uncertainty that resolved, paths that opened because you kept moving. The strength comes from the retrospect, not from what you can currently see ahead.

Keats called this "negative capability": the capacity to stay in uncertainty without reaching for resolution. Not ignorance, not passivity. A form of trust in what you've already navigated.

!!The ground you've crossed is the evidence. The destination is secondary.!!
