2026_07_30 | Character as choice
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Aristotle's concept of prohairesis is usually translated as "deliberate choice" — selecting a means toward an end after deliberation. That's correct but incomplete. Gregory Sadler argues that the standard reading reduces prohairesis to isolated episodes of choosing, missing what's more fundamental: character as a stable disposition that shapes every choice before deliberation begins.

The test cases are akrasia — failing to act on your best judgment — and enkratia — holding to your judgment despite resistance. Both show that prohairesis operates at two levels: as a single act and as a pattern accumulated over time. The choices you make habitually become the character that makes certain choices feel natural or impossible.

[No virtue stands alone](post.html?p=2026/20260622.txt) in Aristotle — and neither does the capacity to choose well.

!!A choice that contradicts your character isn't just a bad decision. It's evidence that the character still needs work.!!
