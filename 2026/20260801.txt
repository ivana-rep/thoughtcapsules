2026_08_01 | Consent you never gave
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Michael Huemer tests the social contract argument by analogy: imagine a private company that claims to own the roads in your region. They demand payment. If you don't pay, they compel compliance. If you resist, they use force. You never signed a contract. But because you continue to live in the area — even if you have no realistic alternative — they claim you've tacitly consented.

If a private actor behaved this way, there's a word for it.

The standard defense of political authority — that by living in a country you've implicitly consented to its rules — relies on the same structure. Huemer's point isn't that the state is equivalent to a criminal organization. It's that [rationalization](post.html?p=2026/20260307.txt) that works backward from a conclusion to a justification isn't consent.

!!The analogy doesn't prove the state is wrong. It proves the standard justification doesn't work.!!
