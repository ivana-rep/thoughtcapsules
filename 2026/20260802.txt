2026_08_02 | Pleasure first

↳ [back to index](index.html)

The standard advice for building a difficult habit is to push through resistance at the start and trust it gets easier. Sarah Perry observed something that inverts this: durable exercise habits form most reliably at intensities the person finds pleasurable, not challenging. Starting at high intensity produces early dropout. Starting where the activity itself is rewarding produces the practice that makes harder effort eventually possible.

The sequence matters. Pleasure doesn't come after mastery — it's the precondition for getting there. Not the optimal version of the activity. The one you actually want to do.

The same logic extends further. The habit that lasts is the one that, at some point, was something you wanted.

!!Discipline built on pleasure is stable. Discipline built on will is fragile.!!
