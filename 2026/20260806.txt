2026_08_06 | Not a lesser friendship
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Aristotle distinguished three kinds of friendship: of utility, built on what someone gives you. Of pleasure, built on what you enjoy together. Of virtue, where you value the other person for what they are. Only the third counts as friendship in the complete sense.

But the first two aren't failures of friendship. They're real and valuable within their own terms. What makes virtue-friendship rare isn't a difference in intention. It's that it requires both people to actually be good, over time, a condition neither one controls alone.

Recognizing someone that way, for what they are rather than what they offer, is close to what Murdoch called [the whole of ethics](post.html?p=2026/20260421.txt).

!!Most friendships don't fail at being complete. They succeed at being exactly what they are.!!
