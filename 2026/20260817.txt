2026_08_17 | Where it's aimed

Complaint can look the same from the outside: same discomfort, sometimes even the same words. What separates murmuring from lament isn't the pain, it's the direction.

Murmuring goes sideways, at whoever's nearby, and it isn't asking for anything to change. It's asking to be agreed with. So nothing moves. Lament goes straight at the source: raw, sometimes accusatory, but addressed. The same split applies to anger. [It stops being spectacle the moment it's aimed at something and asked to do work](post.html?p=2026/20260209.txt).

[Psalm 13](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_13-1-6.txt) runs the whole arc in six verses: unfiltered complaint, then a direct ask, then trust. Not because the situation changed, but because the complaint went somewhere instead of sitting there.

!!The question isn't whether you're allowed to complain. It's whether it's going toward someone who can act, or just landing on whoever's in the room.!!
