2026_08_19 | Working doesn't make it good
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

For the Stoics, guilt is never good. It's a pathology, a judgment distorted by passion, in the same family as shame and remorse. That's a harder claim than it sounds. It means an emotion can be categorically bad and still occasionally produce something worthwhile.

Guilt can register a correct judgment: I was wrong. It can motivate change. But producing a good effect doesn't retroactively make it a good emotion. People describing a hard, punishing stretch of their life as "what I needed" are describing the same structure. The bad thing was a vector for transformation, not a disguised virtue.

[Three selves](post.html?p=2026/20260717.txt) argued for leniency toward the past and strictness toward the present. This adds a harder edge to that: even the strictness shouldn't curdle into guilt. Guilt working, occasionally, isn't the same as guilt being right.

!!Something can change you for the better and still be, in itself, something you shouldn't be doing to yourself.!!
