Avoiding stupidity is more efficient than pursuing brilliance. Eliminating the obvious, avoidable errors returns more than optimizing toward excellence, because most of the gap between where you are and where you could be isn't a missing skill. It's an unforced mistake still being repeated.

[The ceiling](post.html?p=2026/20260709.txt) made the case for devotion past the point where efficiency flattens out. This is the move before that one: efficiency itself has to exist first, and it starts by removing what's actively working against you, not by adding more effort on top of a flawed process.

Most disagreements are also ego dressed as inquiry. Saying "you're probably right" and moving on isn't surrender. It's refusing to spend a scarce resource on a fight that has no payoff either way.

!!The fastest way to get better at almost anything is to stop doing the thing that's obviously working against you. Brilliance can wait.!!
