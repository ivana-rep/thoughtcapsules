2026_08_28 | Enough quiet
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Seneca, Epictetus, and Marcus Aurelius all describe the same maneuver: a deliberate pause between what happens to you and what you do about it. Modern writers have started calling this "the Stoic pause," as if the Stoics owned it.

They didn't. The gap between stimulus and response shows up in nearly every tradition that takes self-governance seriously, because it isn't a technique invented by one school.

Scripture asks for something harder than restraint in that same gap. Be still, and know. Elijah didn't find God in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, only in the silence that came after all of it had passed and finally let something quieter through. The pause isn't just where the reaction hasn't solidified yet. It's where there's enough quiet left to hear what was already speaking.

!!Stillness isn't the absence of an answer. It's the condition for hearing one.!!
