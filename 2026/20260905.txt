2026_09_05 | Not all debts

Moral offsetting says a bad act can be cancelled by a good one large enough to outweigh it: fly, then plant enough trees. [Michael Huemer tests the principle](https://fakenous.substack.com/p/the-offsetting-puzzle) by pushing it toward its worst case. Eat meat, then fund enough vegan outreach to offset the harm — the logic still seems to hold. Kill someone, then fund enough life-saving donations to offset that death — the logic collapses on contact.

The formal argument for offsetting is valid in all three cases. Only intuition tells you the third one is monstrous. That gap points to a line the formal argument can't see: some wrongs are rights violations, and a rights violation isn't the kind of debt a later good deed can pay down. It's the moral version of [what can be corrected and what can't](post.html?p=2026/20260423.txt): a bad act that violates no one's rights can still, in principle, be offset.

!!Not every account can be balanced later. Some debts were never the kind that compounds toward a payoff.!!
