2026_09_07 | Two logics of loss
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Stoic ethics divides everything in life into three bins. Virtue, meaning wisdom, courage, justice, temperance, is the only true good. Vice is the only true evil. Everything else, health, money, comfort, time, even your own life, falls into a third bin: indifferent. Some indifferents are preferred, health over sickness, a friend's company over solitude, but preferred is not the same as good. Nothing in that third bin is worth as much as virtue, and nothing in it is worth less than vice.

That produces an odd consequence. If only virtue and vice are ever truly at stake, then giving up something merely preferred, comfort, a relationship, your own safety, can't really be a sacrifice in the strict sense. You haven't lost a good. You've just traded one indifferent for a better-aligned life. [Gregory Sadler works through exactly this tension](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/would-a-stoic-make-sacrifices): the theory says nothing indifferent is ever really sacrificed, but the daily practice of Stoicism, askesis, requires constantly giving up preferred things anyway, and that giving-up still feels like loss even when the philosophy says it technically isn't one.

Christianity resolves the same puzzle in the opposite direction. It doesn't shrink the loss down to nothing by declaring the thing given up unreal or valueless. It builds its central story on the cost being real and total: [there is no greater love than to lay down one's life for one's friends](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-1-13.txt). The widow praised in the gospels for her offering isn't praised despite giving everything she had to live on. She's praised because of it. The weight of the loss is the measure of the love, not a miscalculation to be corrected.

[No virtue stands alone](post.html?p=2026/20260622.txt) in Stoic ethics either, virtue is a system, not a list, which is its own way of insisting that the architecture has to hold up under real pressure, not just in theory.

!!Stoicism removes the sting of loss by declaring it doesn't count. Christianity keeps the sting, and makes it the measure of what the gift was worth.!!
