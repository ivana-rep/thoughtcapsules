↳ [back to index](index.html)

Objective, impersonal truth, the kind that holds regardless of who's speaking, isn't a default setting of the human mind. [Andrey Mir traces it to a specific technology](https://bigthinkmedia.substack.com/p/as-print-literacy-fades-our-perception): writing, which for the first time separated the knower from the known and made it possible to check a claim against something other than the authority of who said it. Oral cultures didn't lack the concept. They ran on a different one, truth as situational, tied to the person and the moment.

Mir's claim is that digital media are dissolving the separation writing built: immersion and immediacy replacing argument, a kind of orality returning, minus the community that gave the old version its coherence.

[A good skeptical argument was never meant to be believed](post.html?p=2026/20260831.txt). Mir's history says something adjacent: the ground under any confidence about truth was never as fixed as it felt. It just used to be quieter about moving.

!!The sense that truth is simply out there, waiting to be found, is itself a side effect of a technology old enough to feel like human nature.!!
