Tolstoy opened Anna Karenina by noting that happy families resemble each other while every unhappy one is unhappy in its own way. [Joshua Fields Millburn applies the same shape to discontent](https://www.theminimalists.com/p553/): contentment is fairly uniform, but dissatisfaction comes in an almost infinite range of flavors, envy, comparison, unmet expectation, status anxiety, plain craving for something new.

The mechanism underneath is hedonic adaptation: what once felt extraordinary becomes the baseline, and the baseline stops registering as anything at all. [The remedy that softens suffering also erodes joy](post.html?p=2026/20260630.txt); adaptation runs in both directions, which is exactly why the next upgrade never delivers what the last one promised.

!!Dissatisfaction isn't a failure to appreciate what you have. It's a skill, and like any skill, you get better at it the more you practice.!!
