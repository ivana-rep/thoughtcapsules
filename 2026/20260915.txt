2026_09_15 | More than chapter two
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Anselm's ontological argument, in its textbook version: define God as "that than which nothing greater can be conceived." Existing in reality is greater than existing only as an idea. So if God existed only in the mind, you could conceive something greater — the same God, but real. The definition forces its own conclusion. That's chapter 2 of the *Proslogion*, and it's usually treated as the whole argument.

Gregory Sadler says it isn't. What Anselm calls his "single argument" runs through nearly the entire book: not just that God exists, but that God is the highest good, and that everything else is real, and is good, only by depending on that good. Chapter 2 is the opening move, not the full case.

The standard objection to the argument — that you could define anything into existence this way, a perfect island, a perfect anything — attacks the compact formula as if it were the whole claim. It's attacking the label, not what the label was pointing at.

!!Quoting one clever paragraph and calling it "the argument" is usually the reader's shortcut, not the author's actual claim.!!
