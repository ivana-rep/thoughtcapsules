Doing something well enough to keep doing it isn't the same as it being worth doing. Greatness needs the courage to eliminate: cut what you're merely competent at, to make room for the one thing you could be excellent at. [Subtracting the obvious mistake](post.html?p=2026/20260822.txt) already beats chasing brilliance — this is the same move aimed at a skill set instead of an error.

The grass looks greener on the other path only if you're already obsessed with it. Hype-energy and daily-practice-energy aren't the same fuel. An opportunity you wouldn't want to work at on the ordinary days was never really an opportunity.

!!Competence is what makes elimination hard. You don't cut what's failing — you cut what's merely good enough.!!
