Oliver Sacks remembered a firebomb falling behind his family's house during the Blitz, down to the water jets too weak to reach the flames. His brother later told him he hadn't been there. The memory came from a letter, vivid enough that Sacks absorbed it as his own and only learned the truth decades later, after publishing it in *Uncle Tungsten*.

[Anne-Laure Le Cunff explains the mechanism](https://bigthinkmedia.substack.com/p/the-surprising-link-between-false): memory doesn't store, it reassembles. Fragments get pulled together at the moment of recall, and the brain doesn't reliably tag where each fragment came from. Lose the source and something imagined becomes something lived. Researchers call it imagination inflation.

The part worth sitting with is that this isn't a defect. The same associative looseness that lets you recombine fragments into an idea nobody has had is what lets them recombine into an event that never happened. Work linking hippocampal activity to divergent thinking points at one faculty, not two.

[Nostalgia already edits the friction out](post.html?p=2026/20260401.txt), and [the senses interpret rather than record](post.html?p=2026/20260919.txt). This goes further: the editing isn't a tax on remembering. It's the price of being able to invent at all.

!!You cannot have a mind that imagines freely and remembers exactly. It is one faculty running in two directions.!!
