Best practices are exactly what the name says: what works for most people most of the time. Adopting them gets you reliably to the middle of the distribution, which is why they're worth knowing. It is also the ceiling that comes attached.

[Shane Parrish states it without the hedging](https://fs.blog/brain-food/august-30-2026/): a best practice can only ever produce average performance, because it was derived by averaging. Excellence has to come from wherever the average didn't look.

[Competence is what makes elimination hard](post.html?p=2026/20260916.txt). Best practice is competence in packaged form, pre-approved and defensible, which makes it the hardest thing of all to set down. Nobody asks you to justify doing what everyone does.

!!Following the practices that work for everyone is how you become reliably indistinguishable from everyone.!!
