2026_09_24 | The depth the room asks for
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Arne Naess, the Norwegian philosopher who coined deep ecology, separated shallow responses from deep ones. Shallow treats the symptom: send food where there is hunger. Deep asks what arrangement keeps producing hunger there and not elsewhere. Both are answers. Only one answers the question.

[Jonny Thomson turns this into a rule about context](https://miniphilosophy.substack.com/p/the-unspoken-rules-of-how-and-where): every setting for thinking runs on its own rules, and almost none of them are stated out loud. Reading philosophy at a brunch is a category error in one direction; delivering a structural critique when someone asks about your weekend is the same error in the other. The failure isn't the quality of the answer. It's the mismatch between its depth and the space it was given in.

Politics runs on that mismatch. A shallow fix is visible, fast and electorally cheap, which makes it the rational move even where the problem is structural. Nothing in the incentive rewards asking what the arrangement is for.

[The urge to classify before asking why you're classifying](post.html?p=2026/20260805.txt) is the same reflex one level down: a shallow move made in a space that would have repaid a deeper one.

!!A wrong answer is easy to spot. An answer pitched at the wrong depth passes for a right one.!!
