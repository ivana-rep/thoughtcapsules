John Koenig's [*Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows*](https://www.thedictionaryofobscuresorrows.com) invents words for feelings that don't have any. Sonder: the awareness that every passerby is living a life as detailed as your own. Anecdoche: a conversation in which everyone is talking and nobody is listening.

The definitions are the smaller part. What the words do is convert something that felt like a private malfunction into something with a name, and anything with a name can be reported, compared, recognized in someone else. An unnamed feeling is yours alone by default, not because it's rare.

[Jim O'Shaughnessy files the project](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/osv-field-notes-38) alongside others that reveal complexity under an apparently simple surface. That's the right shelf. The feeling was always structured. It was missing the handle.

[Stating a feeling as a fact makes it contestable](post.html?p=2026/20260922.txt). Giving it a name does the opposite: it makes the feeling discussable without turning it into a claim.

!!Some feelings seem unshareable only because nobody has built the word yet.!!
