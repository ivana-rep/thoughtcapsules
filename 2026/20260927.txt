Asked which principle matters most in a philosophy, [Gregory Sadler refuses the question](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/episode-60-what-is-the-most-important). Not because the answer is difficult, but because the question carries a claim inside it: that a tradition has one root and everything else grows out of it.

Stoicism doesn't work that way, and neither does anything else with enough substance to still be read. The parts hold each other up. Pull one out and name it the foundation and you haven't located the core, you've discarded the structure that made the core mean something.

[The Stoics organized the virtues as a system rather than a list](post.html?p=2026/20260622.txt) for this reason, and [treating chapter two of the *Proslogion* as the whole ontological argument](post.html?p=2026/20260915.txt) is the error in miniature. The instinct is always to compress: find the one sentence, keep it, drop the rest.

!!Looking for the single most important principle is how you end up with the one you can quote and none of the ones that make it work.!!
