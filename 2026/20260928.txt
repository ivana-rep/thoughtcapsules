The sweater you haven't worn in three years. The charger for a phone you no longer own. You already decided about both. You just never carried the decision out.

[Joshua Fields Millburn calls this future trash](https://www.theminimalists.com/): objects already discarded in your head and still sitting on the shelf. A cluttered house, read this way, isn't full of things. It's full of unfinished decisions, and the irritation comes from the gap between a choice made and an action skipped.

That much holds for me. Seeing clearly and not acting is exactly where [honesty stops halfway](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=commonplace/do-my-actions-match-my-intentions.txt). But Millburn stops at the shelf. The fix he implies is clearing it, and clearing it closes the decision without asking why the thing came in. For me that question comes first. Excess is rarely about the object: it's usually something bought to fill a silence. [Life is not measured by how much you own](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/luke_12-15.txt), and enough is discernment, not a purge.

[Tolerance for clutter is a personal threshold](post.html?p=2026/20260804.txt). Knowing what you were running from when you bought it isn't.

!!Emptying the shelf closes the decision. It doesn't answer why the thing came in.!!
