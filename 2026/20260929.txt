To judge someone's character, ignore what they say and watch what they do. [Shane Parrish offers it as a rule for reading others](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-13-2026/), but it cuts harder turned inward. Among the quiet sources of unhappiness he lists is one that sounds almost prudent: protecting your potential by never testing it. The unwritten book stays brilliant. The business never launched can't fail. His remedy is deliberately small: the next concrete step, not the big goal.

Most of this fits. Untested potential is perfectionism doing its favorite job, serving as an alibi for inaction, and [the first step's only job is to exist](post.html?p=2026/20260624.txt).

What doesn't fit is the frame underneath. Potential, in Parrish's version, is something you owe yourself to realize, as if your worth were waiting on the output. I don't start from there. [Growth doesn't wait for you to earn it first](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/romans_5-8.txt): my identity isn't in what I produce. The reason to test the potential isn't to prove it. It's that acting on what you see is what honesty looks like.

!!Test it not to find out what you're worth. That was settled before you started.!!
