2026_09_30 | When everyone has the data
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Every NBA team now tracks every player on every possession with the same camera systems. When data was scarce, having it was the edge. Once everyone has it, the edge moves somewhere else.

[Ryan Resch, former assistant general manager of the Phoenix Suns, tells Jim O'Shaughnessy where it went](https://infiniteloops.substack.com/p/how-data-changed-the-nba-ep-327): to the quality of the questions asked of the data, and to a learning culture where coaches take responsibility for a failed plan instead of blaming the players who ran it. Blame shuts down the feedback that would have told you what went wrong.

The part that holds: a mistake owned becomes data, a mistake hidden becomes a pattern. That's how I want to read my own imperfect days, as information rather than verdict, and [asking a better question is a craft](post.html?p=2026/20260521.txt) that only grows where being wrong is safe to admit.

The part that doesn't: in Resch's account, owning the error is justified by the advantage it produces. I'd keep the practice and drop the reason. Admitting a mistake is worth doing because it's true, and it would still be worth doing on a team where it cost you the season.

!!Owning the mistake pays off. That isn't why it's worth doing.!!
