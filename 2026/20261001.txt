Eileen Gu, Olympic champion in freestyle skiing, describes a split mindset: [train with total humility, compete with total confidence](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/september-3-2026). In practice, assume everything can be improved. On the day, assume you're ready. The two attitudes look contradictory. They're sequential, and the failure is swapping them: confidence in training stops you improving, humility in competition makes you second-guess when there's no time left to use the doubt.

The sequence holds. Humility as openness to correction is how growth happens, one small adjustment at a time.

Two parts don't hold for me. "Everything can be improved", taken literally, is a perfectionist's creed, and perfectionism is the alibi I know best for doing nothing. Growth through consistency means accepting the small daily improvement, not auditing everything. And a confidence built on having prepared enough is only as steady as the preparation. Mine has a different ground: my identity isn't in how well I perform or how consistent I've been. [Apart from Him I can do nothing](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-4-5.txt), which turns out to be a steadier footing than readiness.

[Most time is wasted in the wrong posture for the moment](post.html?p=2026/20260616.txt). The posture matters less than what it rests on.

!!Confidence that rests on your preparation is only as steady as your last good week.!!
