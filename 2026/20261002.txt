A standard objection to virtue ethics: if character is the highest good, won't pursuing it cost you money, status, comfort? [Gregory Sadler answers](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/a-preliminary-worry-about-virtue) that the worry isn't specific to virtue ethics, and that the conflict is narrower than it looks. Aristotle's flourishing includes material conditions, and the virtues, practical wisdom above all, are what let you use external goods well. The real clash appears only at the fork: the rare moment when you must choose between being honest and being paid.

The first half holds. [Virtue is the use you make of indifferent things](post.html?p=2026/20260426.txt), and money without judgment tends to go badly.

The second half doesn't, not for me. Integrity isn't something tested only at rare crossroads. It's a practice: every consistent action, however small, is integrity made visible. The forks are daily, just small: the second helping, the extra hour of scrolling, the purchase that fills a gap. And while [external goods are part of flourishing](post.html?p=2026/20260703.txt), I hold them more loosely than Aristotle does. [Life is not measured by how much you own](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/luke_12-15.txt).

!!The big fork is rare. The small ones are daily, and that's where integrity is actually built.!!
