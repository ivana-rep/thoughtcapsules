2026_10_04 | Not A causes B

Crises don't have a cause. They have a matrix of causes: structural conditions, human decisions, feedback loops, accidents of timing. [Gregory Sadler argues](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/complex-causality-in-crises-and-solidarity) that people, policymakers included, prefer the single line, A causes B, because it's easier to act on and to explain. The cost is that it hides who gets hit hardest, and sets the wrong goal: restoring a status quo that already wasn't working for many. [In the second part](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/complex-causality-in-crises-and-solidarity-a0b) he warns against the opposite trap, filling hidden causes with hidden intentions, which is how conspiracy thinking works.

This holds. Refusing the easy story is a form of honesty, and [most disagreements turn out to be about causation](post.html?p=2026/20260327.txt) anyway.

Where I'd push back is on what comes after the analysis. A matrix of causes can become a reason to wait until the matrix is understood, and that is self-awareness stopping short of action again. For me the honest move is the second half: [do my actions match what I've seen?](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=commonplace/do-my-actions-match-my-intentions.txt) Complexity is a reason to act small and close, not a reason to stay still.

!!Mapping every cause is not the same as doing something about one.!!
