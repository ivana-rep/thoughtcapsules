Two ideas sit side by side in [Jim O'Shaughnessy's weekly selection of quotes](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/two-thoughts-6-12-september). Ram Dass: "As long as you have certain desires about how it ought to be, you can't see how it is." And Euripides: the wisest people follow their own direction and listen to no prophet.

The first is true, and it quietly undermines the second. If wanting things to be a certain way bends what you see, then your own direction is the guide most exposed to that bending. Nobody wants anything more insistently than you want things for yourself.

The tradition I stand in starts from the opposite of Euripides: [apart from Him you can do nothing](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-4-5.txt). Not because judgment is worthless, but because a voice outside your own wanting is the only one that can correct it.

!!The judgment you trust most is the one your desires have had the longest to shape.!!
