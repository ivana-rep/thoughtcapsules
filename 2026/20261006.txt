2026_10_06 | The hinge

The past is fixed, "as close to a fact as philosophy can give us." The future is dizzyingly open. [Jonny Thomson](https://miniphilosophy.substack.com/p/the-philosophers-guide-to-uncertainty) describes us as living on the hinge between the two: because nothing you do can be undone, every next choice feels heavy, and the open future turns into anxious overthinking about each step.

The diagnosis is accurate. The weight, though, depends on an assumption: that the outcome rests on you. If it does, every choice carries the whole future and paralysis is rational.

Christianity rejects that assumption. You answer for the next step, not for where all the paths lead. "[Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/matthew_11-28-30.txt)" is not an invitation to stop choosing. It is permission to choose without carrying what was never yours. [You control the next action and nothing else](post.html?p=2026/20260124.txt).

!!A choice you can't undo is only crushing if you think you're holding up the future.!!
