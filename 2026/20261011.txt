"Make your mood, or it makes you." [Shane Parrish pairs it](https://fs.blog/brain-food/september-28-2025/) with a harder observation: we avoid simple things that work because they don't make us look smart. Top performers do embarrassingly basic things, just better than anyone else, while intelligent people invent elaborate alternatives that achieve less.

The second point holds completely. Sleep, movement, a meal eaten slowly, a phone left in another room: the basics are known. The temptation is to design a better system instead of doing them.

The first point needs one change. You don't have to make your mood. You have to stop letting it make your choices. [The feeling rarely comes before the action](post.html?p=2026/20260623.txt). Integrity is acting on who you've decided to be whatever mood shows up, and the mood is free to stay as it is.

!!The simple thing that works rarely needs a better plan. It needs doing.!!
