[James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/april-16-2026) sorts effort into three outcomes. Do the right thing well: big outcome. Do the right thing poorly: big lesson. Do the wrong thing well: big risk.

The ranking is right. Polish hides the wrong thing, and every improvement makes it harder to stop. A clumsy attempt at the right thing at least points the right way.

The scoring is weaker. Clear still values the right thing by what it pays back: an outcome or a lesson. But some right things teach nothing and pay nothing, and they are still worth doing badly, every day, for years. The prophet Micah put the measure elsewhere: [the Lord has told you what is good](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/micah_6-8.txt), to do what is right, love mercy, walk humbly. Faithfulness, not return. [Waiting until you can do it well](post.html?p=2026/20260417.txt) is how the wrong thing, already mastered, keeps winning.

!!The right thing done poorly is still the right thing. It doesn't need to pay to count.!!
