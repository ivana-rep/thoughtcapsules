Keeping your options open sounds free. [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/april-23-2026) names the cost: "Each option requires energy to hold. A shelf full of maybes is often heavier than a hand holding one yes. Put something down."

The advice is right. The reason given is too small. Clear frames it as energy, as if the problem were efficiency. But nobody hoards maybes to save effort. They hoard them because a closed door feels like losing control of what comes next. The open option is insurance against a future you don't trust.

That's why "put something down" is harder than it sounds, and why it isn't solved by better prioritizing. Paul wrote that he had [learned to be content with whatever he had](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/philippians_4-11-13.txt), with plenty or with little, and named the reason in the next line: Christ gave him strength. Contentment there isn't resignation. It's trust that the future doesn't depend on keeping every door ajar. [Enough](post.html?p=2026/20260609.txt) applies to possibilities as much as to things.

!!You can only put the maybes down once you stop needing them to feel safe.!!
