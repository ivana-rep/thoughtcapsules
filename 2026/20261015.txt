2026_10_15 | The consoling illusion

Stanley Kubrick, in [Jim O'Shaughnessy's weekly selection](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/two-thoughts-19-25-april): "If you can talk brilliantly about a problem, it can create the consoling illusion that it has been mastered."

Kubrick is right, and it applies most to the problems you know best: your own. The more precisely you can describe a habit, a fear, a pattern, the more the description feels like progress. Insight becomes the place where change stops. [The basics are usually known](post.html?p=2026/20261011.txt). Understanding them better rarely moves anything.

He calls it an illusion. Scripture calls it something sharper. James: [don't just listen to God's word, do what it says](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/james_1-22.txt), "otherwise, you are only fooling yourselves." Self-knowledge that never turns into action isn't honesty. It's a more articulate way of avoiding it.

!!A problem well described is still a problem.!!
