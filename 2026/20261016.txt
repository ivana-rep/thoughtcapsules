Anthony de Mello, the Jesuit priest, in *The Way to Love* ([quoted by Thomas Oppong](https://postanly.substack.com/p/winning-in-life-starts-with-extreme)): "You must discover work that you do, not for its utility, but for itself." He asks how many activities in your life you do simply because they delight you and grip your soul. "Find them out, cultivate them, for they are your passport to freedom and to love."

The diagnosis holds. Most people can't count many. Exercise is for health, reading for productivity, rest for a better Monday.

The last line undoes it. A passport is useful: you cultivate these activities in order to reach freedom. The utility comes back in through the door it was sent out of. The Christian order is the reverse. [Christ has truly set us free](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/galatians_5-1-26.txt): freedom is given first, not reached through the right hobbies. [God rested on the seventh day](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/genesis_2-2-3.txt) because the work was finished, not to recover from it. Only someone with nothing left to prove can do something for its own sake.

!!Delight isn't the road to freedom. It's what freedom looks like once it's been given.!!
