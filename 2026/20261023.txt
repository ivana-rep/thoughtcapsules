"Good habits stockpile ease. Bad habits postpone pain." [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/october-2-2025) puts the whole economy of habits in two sentences. A good habit costs you up front and gets easier with every repetition. A bad habit feels free today and sends the bill later, with interest.

The second sentence is exactly right. The cost of a bad habit is real, it's just deferred, and that delay is what makes it so convincing.

The first is too optimistic. Some good habits do become easy. Others never do: getting up early, praying when it feels like talking to the ceiling, stopping when you've had enough. If you keep them because they're supposed to get easier, you'll stop the day you notice they haven't. Paul doesn't promise ease. He writes: [let's not get tired of doing what is good; at just the right time we will reap a harvest if we don't give up](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/galatians_6-9-10.txt). He takes for granted that the tiredness stays. What he promises is the harvest. [Growth that runs on feeling good](post.html?p=2026/20261008.txt) ends early. Growth that runs on faithfulness reaches the thousandth day.

!!Not every good habit gets easier. Some just stay worth it.!!
