Joan Didion, in [Jim O'Shaughnessy's weekly selection](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/two-thoughts-14-20-june): "Character — the willingness to accept responsibility for one's own life — is the source from which self-respect springs."

She is right about where self-respect doesn't come from: not from being liked, not from feeling good about yourself. It comes from owning your choices without moving the blame elsewhere.

The weak point is where she places the source. If self-respect comes from character, it lasts only as long as character holds. And character fails: you break a promise, you act against what you believe, you do the thing you swore you wouldn't. At that point you face a bad choice. Either you play down the failure to keep your self-respect, or you admit it fully and lose it. Neither is honest.

Christianity offers a way out of that bind, because it places your worth somewhere failure can't reach. [If we claim we have no sin, we are only fooling ourselves; but if we confess, He is faithful to forgive](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-john_1-8-9.txt). Admitting the failure no longer costs you your worth, so you can admit all of it. [Whatever you tie your life to gets tested the day you fail it](post.html?p=2026/20261009.txt).

!!Only someone whose worth isn't on trial can afford to take full responsibility.!!
