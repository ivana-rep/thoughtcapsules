"Any person capable of angering you becomes your master." [Thomas Oppong](https://postanly.substack.com/p/21-short-rules-for-winning-at-life), who writes a weekly newsletter on philosophy and self-improvement, builds thirteen rules for difficult people on this line of Epictetus, the Stoic philosopher. Two stand out. Empathy isn't condoning, it's understanding: it doesn't excuse the behavior, but it defuses your anger. And don't be a link: negativity spreads through the people who pass it on.

Both are right. [You rarely know what the other person is carrying](post.html?p=2026/20260418.txt), and refusing to pass on what you received already stops something.

The goal is narrower than it looks. Every rule protects your own calm: the difficult person is bad weather to get through undisturbed. Paul asks for more. [Don't let evil conquer you, but conquer evil by doing good](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/romans_12-19-21.txt): if your enemy is hungry, feed them. Not only breaking the chain, but adding a different link to it.

That doesn't mean letting the harm continue. [Forgiveness isn't reconciliation](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=commonplace/forgiveness-isnt-reconciliation.txt): you can give up revenge, want the other person's good, and still keep your distance from someone who keeps hurting you. Grace isn't naivety. The Stoic stays free of the other person. The Christian is asked to do them good, with open eyes.

!!Refusing to pass on the harm is the Stoic part. Passing on something better is the rest.!!
