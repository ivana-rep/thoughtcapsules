[Emilio Dal Bo](https://www.trasumanare.it/p/le-droghe-moderne-di-cui-non-sapevi), who writes an Italian newsletter on personal growth, defines a drug by its job: it changes how you feel on command, when you can't get there on your own. By that measure, plenty of drugs get applause. "Nobody is ashamed of a calendar with no gaps, a phone that's always on, a device that measures their sleep." He offers three questions to spot them. If I take it away, do I get restless? Do I need more and more of it? Do I keep going even when it makes me feel worse? Each of these habits, he argues, keeps away at least one uncomfortable guest: emptiness, inadequacy, waiting, boredom, uncertainty, responsibility, stillness.

The test is good, and the list of guests is the most honest part of it. [Busyness is often a way of keeping silence out](post.html?p=2026/20261022.txt).

Where he stops short is what happens once the guests are let in. His answer is to sit with them: silence, meditation, tolerance. But emptiness isn't always a void to endure. Augustine, bishop of Hippo in North Africa, wrote around the year 400 that his restlessness was a clue: ["You have made us for Yourself, and our heart is restless until it rests in You."](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=saints/augustine-of-hippo.txt) The psalm turns stillness from a test of endurance into a meeting: [be still, and know that I am God](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_46-10.txt).

!!The emptiness you keep medicating may be a hunger with an address.!!
