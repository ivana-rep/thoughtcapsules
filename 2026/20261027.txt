2026_10_27 | Victory, not truth
↳ [back to full archive](2026/2026_full-archive.html)
↳ [back to index](index.html)

Two lines from [Jim O'Shaughnessy's weekly selection](https://newsletter.osv.llc/p/two-thoughts-11-17-october) describe the same reflex. Sigmund Freud: "It is a predisposition of human nature to consider an unpleasant idea untrue, and then it is easy to find arguments against it." Robert Wright, who writes on evolutionary psychology: "Like a lawyer, the human brain wants victory, not truth."

Both are accurate, and the order Freud describes matters most: the verdict comes first, the arguments after. That's also why the usual remedy fails. "Think more carefully" hands the case back to the same lawyer, who will only argue better. A mind can't cross-examine itself for long.

Scripture is blunter about the defendant and more hopeful about the court. [The human heart is the most deceitful of all things](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/jeremiah_17-7-10.txt#v9), Jeremiah writes, and in the next line God says He searches it. The prayer that answers Freud and Wright asks to be examined by Someone else: [search me, O God, and know my heart; point out anything in me that offends You](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_139-23-24.txt). An unpleasant idea becomes bearable once being wrong no longer costs you your standing. [Only someone whose worth isn't on trial can afford full responsibility](post.html?p=2026/20261024.txt).

!!The lawyer can rest once the case is no longer yours to win.!!
