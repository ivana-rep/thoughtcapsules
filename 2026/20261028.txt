"Until death, all defeat is psychological." [Shane Parrish](https://fs.blog/brain-food/october-18-2026/) quotes the line as Marcus Aurelius', though it doesn't appear in the emperor's Meditations. The idea: most limits sit in how you read the situation, not in the situation itself.

Often true. Many defeats are declared before they happen. You give up after the first refusal, or decide a habit is beyond you after three bad days, when the facts would have allowed more.

But the line has two problems. Some defeats don't live in the head: an illness, a loss, a failure that really happened. Telling someone in that place that it's psychological adds blame to the pain. And the line makes one exception, the largest one. "Until death" admits there is a defeat no attitude can reframe.

Christianity answers both. It takes defeat seriously, death included, and refuses to make it final. Paul admits that [our bodies are dying](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/2-corinthians_4-16-18.txt), and still "we never give up", because our spirits are being renewed every day. Then he speaks to the exception directly: [O death, where is your victory?](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-corinthians_15-55-57.txt) That victory comes from outside the mind: "He gives us victory over sin and death through our Lord Jesus Christ."

!!Defeat is real. It just doesn't get the last word.!!
