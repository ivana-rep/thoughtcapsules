2026_10_29 | Its own reward

For the Stoics, explains [Gregory Sadler](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/bonus-discussion-virtue-and-happiness), a philosophy teacher who writes on ancient ethics, happiness is living virtuously. Not virtue plus a few friends and some money, and not virtue as a tool that produces happiness somewhere else. "We're not virtuous in order to be happy." Hence the saying Sadler traces to Seneca: virtue is its own reward.

The second point is right. Goodness practiced for the payoff isn't goodness, and you can tell: it stops when the payoff does. [The right thing still counts when it doesn't pay](post.html?p=2026/20261012.txt).

The weak point is where it puts happiness: inside your own character, built and kept by you. That makes the happy life a personal achievement, as fragile as the character it rests on. Scripture agrees that goodness isn't a means to something else, but it doesn't place happiness in the self. Goodness grows in a person as [fruit of the Spirit](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/galatians_5-1-26.txt#v22): love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Fruit, not trophy. And happiness is found outside the self: [You will show me the way of life, granting me the joy of Your presence](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_16-11.txt). [Augustine](https://ivana-rep.github.io/soul/post.html?p=saints/augustine-of-hippo.txt), who knew the Stoics well, called the happy life "joy in the truth", and the truth he meant was God.

!!Virtue isn't the reward. It grows near the One who is.!!
