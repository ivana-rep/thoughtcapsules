[James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/october-22-2026) quotes the last lines of George Eliot's *Middlemarch*: "the growing good of the world is partly dependent on unhistoric acts; and that things are not so terrible for you and me as they might have been, is half owing to the number who lived faithfully a hidden life, and rest in unvisited tombs." His reading: don't worry about making the history books, worry about making the world better.

Eliot is right, and the line is unusually generous. Most of what holds a family, a street or an office together is done by people nobody will ever quote.

Clear's frame still keeps a ledger, though. Fame is swapped for impact: the hidden life is worth it because of what it does for the world. But many hidden acts have no measurable effect, and the people who did them never learn whether they mattered. If impact is the reason, those acts look wasted.

Jesus gives a different reason: [give your gifts in private, and your Father, who sees everything, will reward you](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/matthew_6-1-18.txt#v4). A hidden life is hidden from people, not from God. Tomorrow, November 1, the Church celebrates All Saints, a feast for all of them, most of whom no calendar names.

!!No life is hidden from the One who saw it.!!
