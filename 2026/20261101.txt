[Thomas Oppong](https://postanly.substack.com/p/this-tree-rule-teaches-the-hardest), who writes a weekly newsletter on philosophy and self-improvement, calls it the "tree rule", after the poet May Sarton: "I think of the trees and how simply they let go, let fall the riches of a season… Imitate the trees. Learn to lose in order to recover, and remember that nothing stays the same for long, not even pain." In winter, he adds, roots go deeper. "You just have to stop holding on so hard. Let the season turn. Trust the roots."

Letting go is a real skill, and autumn is a good teacher. Holding on to what has already gone (a role, a grudge, an old version of yourself) wears you down.

Two things are missing. A tree lets go without fear because spring always comes back; a person needs a reason to believe something will, and "nothing stays the same" cuts both ways. And "trust the roots" leaves open what the roots are in. [Before pushing harder, check what you're rooted in](post.html?p=2026/20261017.txt).

Jesus used the same picture and put a gardener in it: [My Father is the gardener. He prunes the branches that do bear fruit so they will produce even more](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/john_15-1-27.txt). Loss here is someone's work, done with a purpose, and the root has a name: "remain in Me… apart from Me you can do nothing."

!!Letting go is easier when you know whose hands are pruning.!!
