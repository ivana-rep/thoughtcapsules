[Thomas Oppong](https://postanly.substack.com/p/get-these-3-things-right-and-everything), who writes a weekly newsletter on philosophy and self-improvement, builds a whole piece on an idea from the Buddhist teacher Jack Kornfield: in the end, what counts is how well you loved, how well you lived, and how well you let go. Chasing more leaves you empty anyway. Love, in Oppong's reading, is a practice before it is a feeling: being there when it's inconvenient, making room for the hard conversation.

That part holds, and it's the part most people skip. A feeling can't be asked of you on a bad day. A practice can.

The claim about chasing more is also the oldest one. The king in Ecclesiastes built houses, planted vineyards, piled up silver and gold and denied himself no pleasure, then looked at all of it: [it was all so meaningless, like chasing the wind](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/ecclesiastes_2-3-11.txt#v11). Oppong's three questions are a better place to look. They still grade the self, though: how well you loved, lived, let go. The book's own answer, at its very end, points outside the self: fear God and obey His commands. Love that starts there doesn't need to keep checking how well it's doing.

!!Love is a practice, and its center isn't the one practicing it.!!
