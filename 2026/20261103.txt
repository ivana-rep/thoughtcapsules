2026_11_03 | Aimed elsewhere

In [an essay on ambition](https://open.substack.com/pub/emiliodalbo/p/realizzazione-senza-ambizione), the Italian writer Emilio Dal Bo calls it a sickness of the modern soul. Ambition puts fulfillment in a future self, trades what transcends you for what you can see, and feeds a pride that walks over people on the way up. The people who are truly fulfilled, he says, owe it to talent and to stumbling, not to ambition. Not getting what you want is a rare piece of luck, because it forces you to dig down to the roots of the desire.

The diagnosis is accurate. Ambition borrows all its meaning from a day that hasn't come, and the person it promises you'll become is always one step further on.

The cure is weaker. "Live in the present, aligned with yourself" makes the self its own center, the same self whose wanting caused the trouble, and [the judgment you trust most is the one your desires have had the longest to shape](post.html?p=2026/20261005.txt). Paul had ambition to spare. He didn't trade it for presence; he changed its object: [everything else is worthless when compared with the infinite value of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/philippians_3-7-11.txt#v8). Jesus gives the order in one line: [seek the Kingdom of God above all else](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/matthew_6-19-34.txt#v33), and leave tomorrow to tomorrow.

!!Ambition isn't cured by wanting nothing. It's cured by wanting the right thing first.!!
