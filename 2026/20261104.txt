[Shane Parrish](https://fs.blog/brain-food/october-11-2026/), who writes the Farnam Street newsletter on thinking and decisions, opens with a line from the historian Will Durant: "Forget mistakes. Forget failures. Forget everything except what you're going to do now, and do it."

Durant is right about the paralysis. Replaying a mistake changes nothing except how much of today you lose to it, and [leniency toward the past, strictness toward the present](post.html?p=2026/20260717.txt) is a better split than the reverse.

But forgetting can't be done on command, and a mistake that hurt someone isn't yours alone to forget. What frees you isn't erasing the past but having it settled, and settling needs someone with the authority to do it. Scripture puts the forgetting on God's side first: [He has removed our sins as far from us as the east is from the west](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_103-8-12.txt#v12). Only after that comes the line Durant would recognize: [forget all that, it is nothing compared to what I am going to do](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/isaiah_43-18-19.txt).

!!A mistake can be forgotten once it's forgiven. Before that, you're only looking away.!!
