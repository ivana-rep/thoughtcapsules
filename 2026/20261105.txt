[James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/3-2-1/october-8-2026) puts it plainly: the mind follows what you feed it, so better thoughts need better input. When you're off track, five minutes is enough to break the loop: five minutes of exercise, of writing, of conversation. He closes with the physicist Lise Meitner: "Life need not be easy, provided only that it is not empty."

All of this holds. Attention works like appetite, and [systems built to extract a behavior](post.html?p=2026/20260716.txt) count on you not noticing what you eat.

What it leaves open is what "better" means. Better than the feed is a low bar: an hour of good podcasts can still be a way of never being quiet. Paul gives a list with a standard in it: [fix your thoughts on what is true, and honorable, and right, and pure, and lovely, and admirable](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/philippians_4-4-8.txt#v8). And the emptiness Meitner warns about isn't filled by more input. Some of those five minutes can go to silence held open toward God, which is less an input than a presence.

!!A good diet for the mind includes time when nothing comes in.!!
