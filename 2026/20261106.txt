Should a Stoic feel shame? [Gregory Sadler](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/placing-stoic-shame-in-context), a philosopher who teaches the Stoics online, says the wrong answer is to suppress it quickly. Shame is classed among the fears, but it's a knot: a judgment, a story, a habit, a will. The Stoic work is to pull the threads apart. What exactly do I judge as bad? What would follow if people knew? How likely is the harm? Is my reaction in proportion? Patiently, sometimes with another person's help, each thread comes back under your awareness.

It's a good method, better than the two usual moves, drowning in shame or arguing it away.

It stops one step short. Untangling can show that a shame is out of proportion. It can do nothing with the part that's accurate, when what you're ashamed of really was wrong. The Stoic answer is indifference: reputation doesn't matter, the past can't change. Scripture answers with a verdict from outside: [there is no condemnation for those who belong to Christ Jesus](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/romans_8-1.txt), and [even if we feel guilty, God is greater than our feelings](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-john_3-19-20.txt#v20). [Guilt can work and still not be good](post.html?p=2026/20260819.txt); shame can be right and still not be the end of the story.

!!Shame can be true and still not be final.!!
