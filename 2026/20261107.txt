[Gregory Sadler](https://open.substack.com/pub/gregorybsadler/p/bonus-discussion-pleasures-some-need) takes up a question from a listener. Someone who recognizes an addiction, to alcohol for instance, can't reach Aristotle's temperance toward it, because for them the right measure isn't moderation but none at all. Can they still be fully virtuous? Aristotle's own distinction helps: the vicious person denies that what they do is bad, while someone with a "morbid disposition" (*Nicomachean Ethics*, book VII) knows it's an affliction. Steady, disciplined resistance, together with the real pleasure of sobriety, ends up working much like temperance, even if it isn't quite the same thing. And you can give up for yourself a pleasure you don't condemn in others.

That last point is the most humane in the piece, and it's right. [Temperance is several virtues at once](post.html?p=2026/20260808.txt), and one of them is knowing [your own threshold, not everyone's](post.html?p=2026/20260804.txt).

Where it falls short is the source of the strength. "Disciplined resistance" puts the whole weight on the person, and abstinence held up by willpower alone is a fight renewed every day. Paul knew the discipline, [I discipline my body like an athlete](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-corinthians_9-24-27.txt#v27), but he set it next to a promise: [God is faithful… He will show you a way out so that you can endure](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/1-corinthians_10-13.txt). Zero is a measure, and it doesn't have to be kept alone.

!!For some pleasures the right measure is zero. That isn't a lesser virtue.!!
