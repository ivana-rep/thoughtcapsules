In his column for Il Post, the Italian journalist Michele Serra answers a letter from Mattia, 29, adopted from Ethiopia as a child, who has been through panic attacks. Mattia ties Western anxiety to a lack of desire: well-being, once it becomes the finish line, dulls you and makes your thoughts go stale. Serra agrees with the diagnosis and corrects it: material security is still a necessary starting point.

Both are right, and the correction matters. Comfort isn't the enemy. Treating it as the destination is, and [destinations have a way of turning into transit points](post.html?p=2026/20260630.txt).

Neither says what the desire should be for, though. "Want something" is advice a phone answers in seconds, all day long. Augustine described the same restlessness sixteen centuries ago and named its object: [our heart is restless until it rests in You](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=saints/augustine-of-hippo.txt). The psalm puts the order right: [take delight in the Lord, and He will give you your heart's desires](https://thesouldevotional.github.io/soul/post.html?p=bible/psalms_37-4-5.txt). A comfortable life isn't short of desire. Its desire is aimed too low to hold anyone.

!!Security is where desire should start, not where it ends.!!
